## Solo rules (override team-oriented defaults in the skills)

1. **Scope to ~40% of what a 4-person team would attempt.** One wow moment, one user, one flow. Depth wins over breadth even more when solo.
2. **Claude subagents are your team.** Delegate isolated, well-specified pieces (data scraping, seed data, a UI component, the Devpost draft). Keep the core wow logic yourself, because you have to explain it to judges.
3. **Sleep is scheduled, not optional.** For 36h events, block one 4–5h sleep window between ~40% and ~55% of elapsed time. Exhausted solo presenters lose expo judging.
4. **One-click demo.** You drive and talk at the same time, so the demo path must run with zero typing: seeded state, a reset button, `DEMO_MODE=1` fallbacks.
5. **Expo stamina.** At table/expo judging you will repeat the demo 10–30 times. It must be reliable on attempt 30, and your 60-second version must be as sharp as the 3-minute one.
6. **No pitch partner, so use hack-judge more.** Run it at every checkpoint, not every other one.
7. **Use the team-formation session deliberately.** Either stay solo on purpose, or recruit exactly one person whose strengths are your gaps. Don't join a team by drift.

## First-timer rules (until lessons.md has ≥ 2 entries)

1. `lessons.md` is empty, so `principles.md` is all you have. Treat its rules as defaults, not laws, and note where they felt wrong.
2. `hack-judge` is uncalibrated. Treat its scores as rankings (A beats B), not absolute predictions.
3. **Do a dry run before the real event.** Run the full pipeline (`/hack` → `/hack-retro`) on a self-imposed 6–8h mini hackathon, or a small online hackathon, so you go through your first retro before PennApps.
4. Expect everything to take ~2× your estimate the first time. hack-scope's 1.5× multiplier becomes **2×** for you.
